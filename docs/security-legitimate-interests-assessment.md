# CathoScroll — Legitimate Interests Assessment (LIA): Security and Contact Requests

**Controller:** Joshua Argent, trading as CathoScroll  
**Assessment date:** 21 November 2026  
**Status:** Working assessment — controller approval and verification of actual log retention required.

This assessment is limited to security, account protection, abuse prevention and service integrity. It does not establish a legitimate-interests basis for analytics, Personalisation, Sponsor measurement, marketing or processing religious/philosophical belief data.

## 1. Purpose test

CathoScroll has legitimate interests in:

- Protecting user accounts and preventing unauthorised access.
- Detecting and preventing spam, abuse, fraud, automated attacks and service disruption.
- Applying proportionate rate limits to authentication, search, comments, likes, reports and other sensitive endpoints.
- Investigating security incidents and maintaining the integrity and availability of the service.
- Protecting users, sponsors and the platform from malicious activity.

These interests are legitimate, specific and lawful. The processing is not intended to generate commercial advertising profiles or infer religious beliefs.

## 2. Necessity test

Limited technical and security information is necessary to achieve these purposes. Depending on the event, this may include:

- IP address and request timestamp.
- Authentication/session identifiers and account ID where signed in.
- User-agent/device/browser information where needed for security diagnostics.
- Request identifiers, endpoint/action type, rate-limit counters and abuse/security event metadata.
- Reports submitted by users and moderation/security records.

The service should not collect topic-reading history, religious-interest profiles or sponsor engagement for these security purposes. Security records should not be repurposed for Personalisation or sponsor targeting. Where a less intrusive control achieves the same security objective, use it.

## 3. Balancing test

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| User expectations | Users reasonably expect account protection, spam prevention, rate limiting and incident investigation as part of operating an online account and community service. |
| Nature of data | IP addresses, account IDs and device/request metadata can be personal data. They are not intended to be special-category data. User-submitted content may contain sensitive information and must be handled under a separately approved basis/condition. |
| Impact on individuals | Expected impact is limited when collection is restricted to security events and operational metadata. Persistent tracking, cross-service profiling or religious-interest inference is outside this LIA. |
| Safeguards | Minimise fields; restrict access; use short retention; keep security data separate from analytics and sponsor systems; apply RLS/server-side access controls; log privileged access; do not expose security records to sponsors or recommendation systems. |
| Reasonable expectations and control | Explain security processing in the Privacy Policy. Provide the right to object under Article 21 and assess objections individually, recognising that some processing may remain necessary to protect the service and other users. |

## 4. Safeguards and retention actions

1. Confirm exactly which security logs and rate-limit records CathoScroll writes and which are generated by Supabase and Vercel.
2. Record retention for each provider log, authentication log, rate-limit record and abuse report. Do not claim a fixed period until verified in the provider account.
3. Set the shortest retention that still supports incident investigation and abuse prevention.
4. Restrict production access to authorised personnel and review provider team membership.
5. Keep security processing separate from optional Service improvement analytics, Sponsor measurement and Personalisation.
6. Do not use this LIA as an Article 9 condition. If security processing intentionally reveals or uses religious/philosophical beliefs, stop and identify a valid Article 9 condition before proceeding.

## 5. Contact, support and feedback requests

For general contact and feedback requests, CathoScroll has a legitimate interest in receiving and responding to messages about the service, support issues, bug reports and topic suggestions. For account support or steps requested before a service arrangement, Article 6(1)(b) may apply instead.

The minimum information required is normally the sender's name, email address, subject and message. The contact form sends these fields to Formspree for delivery to CathoScroll. The form tells users not to include passwords, payment details or sensitive personal information. Messages are not used for analytics, Personalisation, sponsor targeting or marketing.

The balancing test is favourable where messages are limited to the request, access is restricted, the information is deleted once the request is resolved and no longer needed, and users are informed that Formspree processes submissions. Users would reasonably expect a service contact form to deliver their message to the service operator.

The form must not solicit religious/philosophical beliefs or other special-category data. If a person voluntarily includes such information, do not use it for another purpose. If it is not necessary to respond, delete or redact it. If further processing of special-category data is necessary, identify and document a valid Article 9 condition before doing so; Article 6(1)(f) alone is insufficient.

**Provisional conclusion:** Article 6(1)(f) is a plausible basis for responding to general enquiries, subject to controller approval and the retention/access safeguards above. Confirm the mailbox provider and Formspree retention/deletion terms.

## 6. Conclusion

**Provisional conclusion:** Article 6(1)(f) is a plausible basis for the narrowly defined security and service-integrity purposes above, subject to controller approval, actual-field verification, retention configuration and implementation of the safeguards.

This LIA does not cover analytics, Personalisation, Sponsor measurement, user-generated religious content or any processing that reveals or infers beliefs. Those purposes require their own Article 6 assessment and, where special-category data is processed, a separate Article 9 condition. The intended condition for the optional account-linked purposes is explicit consent under Article 9(2)(a).
